Generated by `uv run python scripts/prepare_data.py` from `data/raw/100-0.txt`. Do not edit these files by hand: change the script and regenerate.

- `works/` holds 44 files, one per work, in the order of the source.
- `split.json` is the frozen train / validation / test split; `scripts/make_split.py` explains the choice and re-checks it.
- `manifest.json` records, for each file, its title, kind, genre, source line range, size and SHA-256. It also records how many lines each cleaning rule touched, and the full alphabet with counts.

## The rule

Remove what an editor or transcriber added. Keep what Shakespeare's readers would see. Never reword a line of verse or prose.

## What was deliberately kept

- **Work titles, cast lists and their `SCENE:` setting lines.** Cast lists were compiled by editors, not by Shakespeare, but they are how a reader meets a play. They are 0.60% of the corpus.
- **Act and scene headings, speaker labels, stage directions**, including the `[_underscores_]` that mark italics.
- **All verse layout:** indentation of 2 or more spaces, blank lines inside a work, sonnet numbers, the roman numerals of *The Passionate Pilgrim*.
- **Dedications, the Argument of *Lucrece*, and the Latin epigraph of *Venus and Adonis*.**
- **Spelling and typography as found:** curly quotes, accented letters, `æ` and `œ`, digit speaker labels such as `1 KEEPER.` in the *Henry VI* plays, and *Much Ado*'s plain-bracket stage directions.
- **Three editor's marks inside lines of verse** (`But . . . .` in *Titus*, `Could … get me.` in *King John*, `_. . . odours` in the *Dream*), because removing them would mean rewording the line.

## What "only Shakespeare" means here

This corpus is the 44 works of Project Gutenberg eBook #100: the traditional canon as published under Shakespeare's name. That is not the same as "every word is his", and we would rather say so than pretend.

- ***The Passionate Pilgrim*** (0.32% of the corpus) is a 1599 miscellany. Only 5 of its 20 poems are securely Shakespeare's, and those 5 are variants of Sonnets 138 and 144 and of three poems in *Love's Labour's Lost*. The rest are by Richard Barnfield, Bartholomew Griffin, Christopher Marlowe, Walter Raleigh and unknown authors.
- **Six plays are widely thought to be collaborations**, about 14% of the corpus by words: *The Two Noble Kinsmen* and *Henry VIII* (with John Fletcher), *Pericles* (George Wilkins), *Timon of Athens* (Thomas Middleton), *Titus Andronicus* (George Peele) and *1 Henry VI*. *Macbeth* also carries passages usually given to Middleton.

These attributions come from general scholarship, not from anything in the file. Pruning by attribution would need scene-level tables that scholars still argue over, and would cost a seventh of an already small corpus. So everything is kept, and none of these works is used to measure the model: the five validation and test works are his alone.

## How this was checked

The script asserts the exact number of lines every rule touches and writes nothing unless all checks pass. Its output was then audited, in two rounds, by independent reviewers who wrote their own tools:

- **Alignment.** All 196,022 raw lines were aligned against the output: 191,267 kept unchanged, 2,874 edited, 1,881 dropped, and every one of them matched to a rule. Across all edits, the only characters changed apart from spaces, tabs and the margin digits were 33 removed (27 ditto marks, 5 brackets, 1 straight apostrophe) and 223 added (219 of them the spelled-out ditto descriptions).
- **Surviving clutter.** A hunt through all 44 files for anything an editor added. Its findings became the second round of rules.
- **Mutation testing.** 110 deliberate corruptions of a copy of the raw file. Every structural one was caught, and every failing run left the existing output untouched.
- **Structure.** Each work checked against what is known about it: five acts per play, 154 sonnets with the known irregular ones, the stanza counts of the narrative poems.
