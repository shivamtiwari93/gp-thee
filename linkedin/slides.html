The rule

Pretend the only text
that ever existed
was Shakespeare.

  • 44 works, ~5 MB, 97 characters. 38 plays, 154 sonnets, 5 poems — and not one other word.
  • Everything built from nothing. The tokenizer, the model, the training loop — no imports from a pretrained world.
  • It reads one character at a time. A comparison with fixed rules chose characters over word-fragments.
GP-Thee-11M · a closed universe of language2 / 5
The hard part wasn't the model

It was measuring it
honestly.

  • Three works locked away on day one. A history, a romance, a poem — never trained on, never looked at.
  • Every rule written down before the number. Which run to release, what counts as memorising — fixed in advance, in the open.
  • A one-way door. The held-out works could be scored exactly once. A score is only held out until you use it.

What it is, and why

An in-character autocomplete
for a world of only
Shakespeare.

It can't answer a question or follow an instruction. Give it a speaker and a line and it plays the next part. I built it for one reason: to see whether a language model could be made, and measured, honestly — every number checked before it was written. It could.
